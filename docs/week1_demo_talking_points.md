# ProspectLens video: talking points, not a script

Aim for roughly 4 minutes 30 seconds, leaving room to speak naturally. Choose the
ideas that match your experience; you do not need to cover every feature. The
suggested times are a flexible rehearsal guide, not text to read aloud.

## Before recording

Open the app, this outline, the repository and one real prompt screenshot. Use
Manage data to restore the synthetic demo, reset filters and close private tabs.
Keep the browser large enough for chart labels. Check microphone and notification
settings. Do not show real contact details or claim localhost is a public app.

## 0:00–0:25 — Why this project?

Show the dashboard introduction.

- What consulting or spreadsheet problem were you trying to make easier?
- Who would use ProspectLens, and what question could they answer faster?
- Add your own reason for choosing this project; this is the strongest place to
  make the demo personal.

## 0:25–0:50 — Where does the data come from?

Show the source indicator, then briefly a blank template or dictionary.

- Mention 150 synthetic survey responses and 35 discovery assessments.
- Explain that a prospect ID is like a business folder containing several forms.
- Distinguish fictional demonstration data from real market research.
- Optional analogy: the schema is the agreed format that stops columns being
  interpreted differently each time someone uploads a spreadsheet.

## 0:50–1:40 — Explore a market segment

Stay in Market Intelligence. Select one industry and show the KPI/chart changes.
Hover a bar or open View counts and denominators; reset the filter afterwards.

- What changed when you selected the industry?
- Explain the difference between a respondent count and a percentage.
- Use the example of one business selecting three challenges: three category
  mentions, still one business in the denominator.
- Optional: briefly show the AI-adoption heatmap or its donut selector if time allows.

## 1:40–2:45 — Inspect one business

Switch to Opportunity Intelligence. Hover a matrix bubble, click it to open
Business details, and point to one score component. Close the pop-up; show How
scores work only if useful rather than opening every section.

- Horizontal axis: commercial evidence. Vertical axis: significance of the problem.
- Why can a serious problem still have weak evidence of a paid engagement?
- Explain that 11 businesses have enough evidence for an overall score; the other
  businesses remain in the ranking rather than receiving invented zeroes.
- Use one example from the visible record, not a memorised score that may change
  with data or model settings.
- Mention that provisional scores help decide what to investigate, not predict sales.

## 2:45–3:10 — Explain activity over time

Scroll to Opportunity Discovery Over Time. Switch Cumulative to New Per Period,
then hover a point. Use Weekly grouping unless a monthly view makes your point clearer.

- Cumulative is the running total; New Per Period is what was added in that period.
- The lines show data collection and assessment activity, not growing market demand.
- If relevant, mention that you requested straight segments and markers because
  the original staircase was harder to read; the numbers did not change.

## 3:10–3:40 — Turn the view into an output

Download the ranking or shortlist and open it. Choose one export to demonstrate,
rather than spending the time opening all three.

- How would a consultant use this after a meeting or research session?
- Explain that the export follows the selected filters.
- If showing the shortlist, point to Analytical only and explain that interest
  in follow-up is not documented permission to contact.
- If showing the brief, acknowledge its current wording caveats instead of
  presenting it as a fully polished client recommendation.

## 3:40–4:15 — What did you learn from building it?

Show a real prompt and resulting change, or the repository and test output.
Choose one story, such as:

- Asking for shared data management after noticing the two lenses could use
  different uploads.
- Refining a chart based on what you could actually read, rather than accepting
  the first generated design.
- Finding a download problem through browser testing, despite correct formulas.
- Asking for an independent export audit instead of assuming a download was accurate.

Explain your own role: what you asked, what you checked, what you understood and
what still needed the assistant's help. You can mention the 202-test result from
12 October 2026; if you run more tests later, update this number from the output.

## 4:15–4:35 — Close with one useful next step

- Name the part you are most satisfied with and why.
- State one limitation: synthetic data or provisional scoring are clear examples.
- Pick one next step, such as consultant feedback or clearer brief wording.
- Point to the repository; add a public app link only if it has actually been deployed.

## Personal preparation prompts

Write a few words, not full sentences, beneath these questions before recording:

- The moment I realised a change was needed was…
- One thing I can now explain without the assistant is…
- A suggestion I questioned or corrected was…
- The next thing I would build or validate is…

Record one practice take. If it exceeds five minutes, remove an optional chart
or shorten the template explanation rather than rushing through every interaction.
